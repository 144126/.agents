# Music glossary for Suno prompts

Suno's official glossary, for when a prompt needs a precise word instead of a vague adjective. Any of these can go straight into the Style field.

Source: https://help.suno.com/en/articles/9010177

## Tempo & rhythm

- **Tempo** — speed, measured in BPM
- **Adagio** — slow, 66–76 BPM ("at ease")
- **Andante** — moderate walking pace, 76–108 BPM
- **Allegro** — fast, lively, 120–168 BPM
- **Presto** — very fast, 168–200 BPM
- **Rubato** — flexible tempo, speeding and slowing expressively
- **Syncopation** — emphasis on normally weak beats or off-beats
- **Polyrhythm** — two or more conflicting rhythms at once
- **Groove** — the rhythmic feel or "pocket" that makes you move
- **Downbeat** — first beat of a measure, typically strongest
- **Upbeat** — the beat before the downbeat, creates anticipation

## Dynamics & expression

- **Dynamics** — volume or intensity
- **Crescendo** — gradually louder
- **Diminuendo / Decrescendo** — gradually softer
- **Forte (f)** — loud · **Fortissimo (ff)** — very loud
- **Piano (p)** — soft · **Pianissimo (pp)** — very soft
- **Accent** — emphasis on a note or beat
- **Staccato** — short, detached notes
- **Legato** — smooth, connected notes
- **Vibrato** — slight pitch variation adding warmth
- **Tremolo** — rapid repetition of a note or alternation between notes

## Song structure

- **Intro** — opening section, establishes mood
- **Verse** — tells the story, lyrics change
- **Pre-Chorus** — transitional build into the chorus
- **Chorus** — main repeated section, central message
- **Bridge** — contrasting section, variety and tension
- **Refrain** — repeated line, often ending verses
- **Hook** — catchy, memorable musical or lyrical phrase
- **Break** — instruments drop out for contrast
- **Drop** — in electronic music, the moment of maximum energy release
- **Outro** — closing section

## Melody & harmony

- **Melody** — the main tune
- **Harmony** — simultaneous notes supporting the melody
- **Chord** — three or more notes together
- **Chord Progression** — sequence of chords forming the harmonic foundation
- **Key** — tonal centre based on a scale
- **Major** — bright, happy · **Minor** — darker, sadder
- **Scale** — notes in ascending or descending order
- **Interval** — distance between two pitches
- **Octave** — interval to a note of double the frequency
- **Arpeggio** — chord notes played in sequence
- **Counterpoint** — independent melodies played together
- **Dissonance** — tension from clashing notes
- **Resolution** — dissonance moving to consonance

## Genres & styles

Blues · Jazz · Rock · Pop · Electronic/EDM · Hip-Hop · R&B · Country · Classical · Folk · Funk · Soul · Reggae · Metal · Ambient

Sub-genres beat these broad labels in a prompt — `memphis soul`, `liquid drum and bass`, `desert blues` target far better than the parent genre.

## Instrumentation & texture

- **Instrumentation** — which instruments are used
- **Arrangement** — how parts are organised
- **Texture** — overall sound quality from combined elements
- **Monophonic** — single melodic line, no accompaniment
- **Homophonic** — melody with harmonic accompaniment
- **Polyphonic** — multiple independent melodic lines
- **Orchestration** — assigning musical elements to instruments
- **Timbre** — the colour of a sound (guitar vs piano)
- **Layering** — stacking sounds for richness
- **Sparse** — minimal, space between elements
- **Dense** — many elements at once

## Vocal techniques

- **Falsetto** — high, airy register above normal range
- **Belt** — powerful sustained chest voice at high pitch
- **Melisma** — multiple notes on one syllable
- **Vocal Run** — quick succession of notes, often improvised
- **Harmonization** — voices on different notes simultaneously
- **A Cappella** — no instrumental accompaniment
- **Call and Response** — one phrase answered by another
- **Scat** — improvised vocals on nonsense syllables (jazz)
- **Crooning** — soft, intimate style
- **Rapping** — rhythmic spoken or chanted lyrics

## Production & effects

- **Reverb** — simulated space (room, hall, cathedral)
- **Delay / Echo** — sound repeated after an interval
- **Compression** — reduced dynamic range
- **Distortion** — grittier, heavier alteration
- **Filter** — removes or emphasises frequencies
- **Modulation** — variation in pitch, amplitude, or other parameters
- **Panning** — position in the stereo field
- **EQ** — balance of frequency components
- **Sampling** — using existing recordings in new compositions
- **Loop** — repeated section
- **Fade In / Out** — gradual volume change at start or end

## Advanced concepts

- **Modulation (key change)** — shifting key mid-piece
- **Time Signature** — rhythmic framework (4/4, 3/4, 6/8)
- **Cadence** — formula creating resolution or pause
- **Ostinato** — repeated pattern or phrase
- **Pedal Point** — sustained note while harmonies change above
- **Augmentation** — lengthening rhythmic values
- **Diminution** — shortening rhythmic values
- **Suspension** — note held from one chord into the next
- **Anacrusis** — pickup notes before the first full measure
- **Coda** — concluding section
